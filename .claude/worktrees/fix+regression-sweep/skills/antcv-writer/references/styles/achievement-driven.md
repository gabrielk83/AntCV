# Style: Achievement-Driven

**Primary constraint.** Outcome-first ordering. Lead with what changed because of you.

**Row in style-matrix.md.** Density `medium`. Words per bullet 12 – 18. Profile 260 – 340 chars. ATS-Modern native-safe; "Key Achievements" normalised to "Achievements" in legacy.

---

## What this style sounds like

Every bullet begins with what changed, then names the work that produced the change. Numbers carry weight where they are real and defensible — but absent numbers do not get faked or padded with adjectives. The voice is direct, scope-anchored, and assumes the reader is scanning for impact before responsibilities.

This style suits senior commercial roles, sales, product management, and leadership positions where the hiring decision turns on what the candidate has produced. It does not suit early-career roles (where what you did matters more than what changed yet) or research roles (where outputs are framed differently — use `research-formal`).

---

## Section-by-section

### `profile`

Two to three sentences. 260 – 340 characters. Lead with role and impact, not adjectives.

- Sentence 1: role + domain + scope marker (years, geography, P&L if applicable).
- Sentence 2: one or two concrete outcomes that anchor the candidate's level.
- Sentence 3 (optional): current focus framed as what to scale or deliver next.

**Good:**

> Product manager with 12 years leading hardware programmes in automotive perception. Closed two ASPICE re-certifications, cut customer review cycles 40%, and shipped LiDAR system architecture across three tier-1 customer programmes. Now scaling change governance for next-generation perception stacks.

**Avoid:** opening with adjectives or values claims. Outcome-soup without scope ("achieved many results across many domains"). Empty scope ("delivered impact at scale").

### `core_competencies`

Default format here is `bullets` rather than `table-grid` — outcome-oriented styles read better as a vertical list. 5 – 7 bullets, each a focus area followed by the candidate's specific angle.

**Good:**

- **Change governance** — Multi-vendor change boards across three tier-1 customer programmes; ASPICE-aligned.
- **System architecture** — Hardware-software interface ownership; requirements traceability adopted by two downstream teams.
- **Programme delivery** — Customer change requests closed under deadline; cross-supplier negotiation without escalation.
- **Functional safety** — ISO 26262 assessor; led two re-certifications with zero major findings.

**Avoid:** Generic focus areas without the candidate's specific angle ("Project management — strong skills in planning"). Repeating the same outcome across multiple rows.

### `selected_outcomes`

This is the centrepiece section in `achievement-driven`. 4 – 6 bullets. Each title is an outcome in 3 – 6 words; each body is the context, action, and quantification.

**Title rules:**

- Outcome, not topic. "Cut review cycles 40%" not "Worked on review cycles".
- Numbers in the title when honest. "Closed two CCRs under deadline" not "Closed several CCRs successfully".
- Verb at start, result at end where possible.

**Body rules:**

- 1 – 2 sentences. 12 – 20 words total.
- Context (what the situation was), action (what you did), result (what changed) — in that order.
- Quantify the result if a real number is available. If not, write the result without a number rather than padding with adverbs.

**Good:**

- **Cut review cycles 40%.** Pre-screened customer change requests against ASPICE checklist before formal board, reducing average closure time from 25 days to 15.
- **Two ASPICE re-certifications, zero findings.** Drove preparation across optical, electrical, and software teams; coordinated assessor reviews; closed all open items pre-audit.
- **Shipped LiDAR system architecture to three tier-1 customers.** Owned hardware-software interface specification, requirements traceability, and customer integration handover.

**Avoid:** Outcomes without action ("Improved the team"). Actions without outcomes ("Led customer reviews"). Magnitude words instead of numbers ("Significantly reduced cycle time").

### `experience`

3 bullets per role at `target_pages` ≤ 1.5; 4 – 5 at longer pages. Each bullet leads with outcome or scope.

**Bullet structure for this style:**

- Bullet 1: scope of role — what was owned (revenue, headcount, geography, technical surface).
- Bullet 2: one or two key actions that drove outcomes.
- Bullet 3: the outcome that resulted, with a metric if available.

**Good (System Architect, Innoviz, 2020 – 2025):**

- Led change control across three automotive tier-1 customer programmes; owned customer change request closure and system architecture handover.
- Built requirements traceability between optical subsystem and ECU software teams; introduced pre-board ASPICE screening to reduce review cycles.
- Cut average review cycle time 40%; closed two CCRs under deadline without escalation; supported two ASPICE re-certifications with zero major findings.

**Avoid:** Bullets that only describe responsibilities ("Was responsible for change control"). Bullets that name an action without an outcome.

### Other sections

`tools_methods`, `certifications`, `education`, `publications_patents`, `additional_information` follow `cv-skeleton.md` defaults. Achievement-Driven does not change these — the outcome focus lives in the main column.

## Banned forms specific to achievement-driven

In addition to the global banned word/phrase lists, this style rejects:

- Magnitude words as a substitute for numbers: `significantly`, `substantially`, `considerably`, `markedly`. If a number is available, use it. If not, write the outcome qualitatively.
- "Was responsible for" / "Responsibilities included" / "Tasked with" — replace with the outcome.
- Generic outcome claims: "delivered value", "drove impact", "moved the needle".
- Hedging on outcomes: "helped to reduce", "contributed to improvement in", "supported delivery of". Either you did it or you assisted — name which.
- Scope-without-outcome: "Led a team of 10" by itself is not an outcome. Pair it with what the team delivered.

---

## Preferred forms

- **Outcome-first verbs in titles.** Cut, Closed, Shipped, Doubled, Reduced, Saved, Won, Built, Launched.
- **Number + noun in the title.** "40% review-cycle reduction" or "Two ASPICE re-certifications". Specific count or percentage anchors the claim.
- **Scope markers in bullet 1 of each role.** "Across three tier-1s" / "Eight direct reports" / "$12M annual budget" / "EMEA region".
- **Comparative anchoring when honest.** "From 25 days to 15." "From four review rounds to two." Specific delta beats abstract improvement.
- **Negative result claims when relevant.** "Zero major findings". "No escalations". "No customer-reported regressions". These are valid outcomes.

## JD signals that suggest this style

Recommend `achievement-driven` when the JD shows any of:

- Senior or director-level role with explicit P&L, headcount, or scope language.
- "Drive results", "Deliver impact", "Scale the function" in the JD (these phrases are recruiter-side, not candidate-side — the JD is telling you the company values outcome framing).
- Sales, marketing, growth, or revenue-attached product management roles.
- Companies known for outcome-based evaluation cultures (Amazon, Meta, top-tier consulting).
- The JD lists specific KPIs the role owns.

Do **not** recommend `achievement-driven` when:

- The role is early-career (junior, associate, graduate, intern). Use `measured-professional` or `cold-outreach`.
- The role is operational/process-focused (ops, quality, compliance). Use `structured-professional`.
- The candidate's history genuinely lacks outcome data — the style will read as forced.
- Academic or research roles. Use `research-formal`.

## Compression behaviour

When over budget:

1. Compress bullet bodies before dropping bullets. The outcome title is sacrosanct; the body can lose context sentences.
2. `experience` bullet 3 (the outcome) is the priority; bullets 1 and 2 (scope, action) compress first.
3. Profile compresses by removing sentence 3 (forward-looking), then by tightening sentence 2.
4. `core_competencies` drops the specific-angle clause before dropping a row entirely.

Compression priorities to preserve: outcome titles, quantified metrics, scope indicators, domain nouns specific to the role.

---

## Tone-chip compatibility

Style defaults to `["outcome-led", "quantified", "scope-anchored"]`. Additional compatible chips: `precise`, `concrete`, `institutional` (for prestige contexts), `scope-defined`.

Conflicts: never accept `narrative`, `method-led`, `why-led` — they reorder the bullet against the primary constraint.
