# Style: Context Rich

**Primary constraint.** Narrative voice. Say why this work mattered, not just what was done.

**Row in style-matrix.md.** Density `high`. Words per bullet 17 – 26. Profile 360 – 500 chars. ATS-Modern native-safe — narrative content parses fine.

---

## What this style sounds like

Sentence-shaped bullets, not fragments. Longer profile paragraphs that argue for the candidate's trajectory rather than catalogue it. Reasoning clauses are welcome — the bullet says what was done and why that mattered. The reader is expected to be thoughtful and to have time.

This style fits humanities, education, NGOs, sustainability, communications, policy, journalism-adjacent roles, and career-transition moments where the why behind a move needs space to land. It is the wrong choice for transactional commercial roles where the reader is scanning for impact in five seconds.

The voice carries more of the candidate's interpretation than other styles. "I joined this organisation because" is acceptable in the profile. "This experience shaped" is acceptable in a transition bullet. The candidate is allowed to think on the page.

---

## Section-by-section

### `profile`

Two paragraphs or one long paragraph. 360 – 500 characters. Argues for a trajectory rather than listing roles.

- Sentence 1: role and domain, but framed with a why or a how — what kind of work this person does and why.
- Sentence 2: the through-line in the career, expressed as a question they keep coming back to or a kind of problem they take on.
- Sentence 3: current focus, framed as continuation rather than start.

**Good:**

> Education researcher and programme designer with twelve years in policy-adjacent work, focused on how learning environments shape who gets to participate in scientific careers. My work has moved between universities, NGOs, and ministries — always around the question of access. I am currently developing assessment frameworks for STEM widening-participation programmes in Northern Europe.

**Avoid:** opening with credentials ("PhD in X with experience in Y"). Listing roles in the profile (that is the experience section). Closing with what you want next without grounding why.

### `core_competencies`

Default format here is `bullets` with one-line narrative per item, not the table-grid used by commercial styles. 4 – 6 items.

**Good:**

- **Programme evaluation** — Designed and ran mixed-methods evaluation for three EU-funded widening-participation programmes, with findings adopted by two host institutions.
- **Curriculum co-design** — Worked with secondary-school teachers in Denmark and Sweden to design pre-university physics curricula that opened the subject to non-traditional students.
- **Policy translation** — Wrote ministry-facing briefs that connected classroom-level evidence to national assessment policy.

**Avoid:** one-word focus areas without context — the table-grid style is for `measured-professional`, not for this.

### `selected_outcomes`

3 – 5 bullets. Each title is an outcome described in sentence shape, not a noun-phrase fragment. Body 18 – 26 words, naming what was learned or what changed and why it mattered.

**Good:**

- **Evaluation framework adopted by two host institutions.** After two years running the EU widening-participation programme, the host universities asked me to translate the methodology into a standing framework — now used in their internal programme reviews.
- **Curriculum redesign opened upper-secondary physics to two new school cohorts.** A two-year teacher-researcher collaboration produced materials that schools adopted independently of our team, signalling that the redesign was sustainable beyond the funded period.

**Avoid:** outcome titles in noun-phrase form ("Curriculum framework"). Bullets that name the outcome without the context that makes it meaningful.

### `experience`

3 – 4 bullets per role. Each 18 – 26 words. Past tense for past, present for current. Sentence-shaped.

**Bullet structure for this style:**

- Bullet 1: what the role was and what the candidate took on.
- Bullet 2: an action or programme of work, with the why included.
- Bullet 3: an outcome or learning, framed as what changed or what the candidate now carries forward.

**Good (Programme Researcher, EU-funded Widening Participation Programme, 2018 – 2022):**

- Led the evaluation strand of a four-year EU programme bringing university physics to upper-secondary schools across three Nordic countries.
- Designed a mixed-methods evaluation that combined teacher interviews with student-trajectory tracking, because the funder's KPIs were not catching what the programme was actually doing.
- The framework was adopted by both host universities for their standing programme reviews, and the underlying question — what makes a STEM pathway sustainable for non-traditional students — has shaped my work since.

**Avoid:** fragment bullets. Bullets that strip the reasoning to fit a length target. Bullets that read as duty-summaries.

### Other sections

`tools_methods` and `additional_information` follow `cv-skeleton.md` defaults; this style does not change their format. `publications_patents` is often substantial in context-rich careers — surface where applicable.

## Banned forms specific to context-rich

In addition to the global banned word/phrase lists, this style rejects:

- Fragment bullets. Every bullet is a sentence.
- Telegraphic shorthand: "Led X, owned Y, delivered Z." Use sentences with connecting tissue.
- Outcome-only bullets without context. The reader needs to know why the outcome mattered.
- Commercial-style scope markers as a substitute for meaning: "Across three regions, twelve schools, two ministries" — fine as detail, not fine as the whole bullet.
- "Significant" / "considerable" / "extensive" as stand-ins for specificity.
- Empty learning claims: "This experience taught me valuable lessons in leadership." Either name the specific learning or drop the clause.

---

## Preferred forms

- **Sentence-shaped bullets** with conjunctions, subordinate clauses, and reasoning. "I designed X because the existing Y did not capture Z." This sounds natural and is the register's strength.
- **Why-led framing** in profile sentences and experience bullet 2: "I joined this work because", "I took this on because", "The question that drives my work is".
- **Concrete naming** of institutions, programmes, regions — context-rich does not mean vague. "The EU widening-participation programme across three Nordic countries" beats "an EU-funded programme".
- **Transition language** when career moves matter: "Moved to X because" / "The thread that connects" / "This experience shifted my focus toward". The style allows the candidate to argue for their trajectory.
- **Modesty markers used precisely.** "Part of a team that" / "Co-led" / "Contributed to" — when accurate, these signal precision rather than weakness.

## JD signals that suggest this style

Recommend `context-rich` when the JD shows any of:

- Humanities, social sciences, education, NGO, policy, sustainability, communications, advocacy.
- The JD itself is written in narrative form — multiple paragraphs, full sentences, motivation language, mission framing.
- Roles asking for written communication as part of the job (briefing notes, policy memos, programme reports).
- Career-transition contexts where the candidate is moving between sectors and needs the room to argue for the move.
- Senior practitioner roles in non-commercial sectors (programme director at an NGO, head of public engagement at a university).

Do **not** recommend `context-rich` when:

- The reader is scanning for impact metrics. Use `achievement-driven`.
- The role is operational. Use `structured-professional`.
- The candidate's history fits commercial-outcome framing better than narrative framing.
- Cold outreach. Use `cold-outreach` (the narrative voice will read as overwrought in a 30-second scan context).

---

## Compression behaviour

This style compresses badly. Sentence shape is the style; trimming it produces fragments and the bullet stops sounding like the register.

When over budget:

1. Drop the lowest-priority bullet entirely. Do not shorten kept bullets to fragments.
2. Profile compresses by removing sentence 3, then by tightening sentence 2's reasoning clause.
3. `core_competencies` reduces from 6 items to 4 before changing format.
4. Never convert sentence-shaped bullets to fragment bullets to fit a length target. If forced, switch the style to `measured-professional` and regenerate.

Compression priorities to preserve: narrative threads, reasoning clauses, context sentences that explain why the work mattered.

---

## Tone-chip compatibility

Style defaults to `["narrative", "reasoned", "why-led"]`. Additional compatible chips: `warm`, `relational`, `concrete`, `formal` (raises register without losing narrative shape), `polished`.

Conflicts: never accept `restrained`, `brief`, `factual` (as the global chip — context-rich is not "unfactual", but the chip's effect strips evaluative adjectives that the style needs), `outcome-led`, `method-led`. These flatten the register.
