# Style: Prestige Structured

**Primary constraint.** Institutional weight. Frame every bullet at the scope appropriate to the level.

**Row in style-matrix.md.** Density `high`. Words per bullet 17 – 26. Profile 360 – 500 chars. ATS-Modern native-safe; "Career Highlights" normalised to "Achievements" in legacy.

---

## What this style sounds like

Polished, careful, institutional. Bullets reference scope (revenue, headcount, geographic remit, P&L) where applicable, in formal vocabulary. Longer than other styles — the weight comes from the substance, not from compression. The reader is a senior partner, a head of search, or a board secretary, and the document signals that the candidate has played at the level the role requires.

Where `achievement-driven` says what changed, `prestige-structured` says what scope was held and what came of holding it. The two are adjacent but not the same. Prestige does not bend toward outcome-first ordering; it accepts outcome, scope, or method as the lede depending on which carries more institutional weight.

This style fits senior consulting (McKinsey, BCG, Bain tier), senior finance (banking, private equity, asset management), executive search, board advisory, and senior in-house counsel roles. It does not fit early-career positions or technical individual-contributor roles where the vocabulary reads as overdressed.

## Section-by-section

### `profile`

Two to three sentences, substantive. 360 – 500 characters. Lead with scope-defining role and institutional context.

- Sentence 1: role, years, sector, with scope marker (revenue, AUM, headcount, geographic remit).
- Sentence 2: substantive expertise or institutional positioning.
- Sentence 3 (optional): current focus or strategic context.

**Good:**

> Senior managing director with eighteen years across global financial services, leading multi-jurisdictional restructuring programmes for institutional banking clients with combined balance sheets exceeding €40bn. Built and led a forty-person cross-border advisory function spanning EMEA, with primary client relationships at the C-suite level. Currently chairing the firm's regulatory-affairs steering committee.

**Avoid:** opening with adjectives ("Seasoned executive"). Scope without anchoring numbers ("Led large-scale programmes"). Soft trajectory framing ("Looking to take on broader leadership").

### `core_competencies`

`structured-grid` format — denser than other styles, two or three columns possible. 5 – 7 rows. Each row carries focus area + the candidate's substantive angle in formal vocabulary.

**Good:**

| Focus area | Strategic expertise |
|---|---|
| Multi-jurisdictional restructuring | Lead advisor on three institutional banking restructurings across EMEA, combined balance sheets €40bn+ |
| Regulatory governance | Chair of the firm's regulatory affairs steering committee; principal contact with three national regulators |
| Cross-border advisory leadership | Built and led a forty-person EMEA advisory function across six jurisdictions |
| Board and C-suite engagement | Primary relationship lead for institutional clients at chair-and-CEO level |
| Capital and balance-sheet structuring | Senior advisor on €12bn syndicated capital raise; co-led on two subsequent transactions |

**Avoid:** generic focus areas ("Leadership"). Bullets without scope markers.

### `selected_outcomes`

4 – 6 bullets. Each title 5 – 9 words; body 17 – 26 words. Each bullet anchors the scope at the level appropriate to the role.

**Good:**

- **Lead advisor on €40bn institutional banking restructuring.** Coordinated across three jurisdictions and four regulatory authorities; delivered the structuring opinion that anchored the client's recapitalisation; sustained advisory relationship through subsequent transactions.
- **Built EMEA cross-border advisory function from inception to forty professionals.** Defined the operating model, recruited the senior team, and established the governance framework that the practice continues to operate under five years later.
- **Chaired the regulatory affairs steering committee through three regulatory cycles.** Coordinated the firm's response to the EBA capital framework revisions and represented the firm in industry consultations across six jurisdictions.

**Avoid:** outcome titles in fragment form. Bullets without scope anchoring. Casual register ("Crushed the numbers").

### `experience`

3 – 4 bullets per role. Each 17 – 26 words. Past tense for past, present for current. Substantive and scope-anchored.

**Good (Senior Managing Director, Global Financial Advisory, 2018 – 2025):**

- Led the EMEA institutional restructuring practice, with direct responsibility for forty advisory professionals across six jurisdictions and combined client balance sheets exceeding €40bn.
- Served as principal advisor on three landmark restructuring programmes, including a €12bn syndicated capital raise; sustained primary client relationships at the chair-and-CEO level throughout.
- Chaired the firm's regulatory affairs steering committee through three EBA regulatory cycles, coordinating the firm's response and representing it in industry consultations across six jurisdictions.

**Avoid:** "Was responsible for" — use the institutional role. Bullets without scope. Casual register.

### Other sections

`tools_methods` is typically de-emphasised in this style — replaced or supplemented by `affiliations` (board roles, professional memberships, regulatory designations) and `engagements` (client roster anonymised). Follow `cv-skeleton.md` defaults for the other sections; do not surface tools at the same prominence as commercial-style CVs.

## Banned forms specific to prestige-structured

In addition to the global banned word/phrase lists, this style rejects:

- Casual register: "Got the deal done", "Crushed the targets", "Smashed the goals".
- Startup vocabulary: "Hustled", "Scaled rapidly", "Moved fast and broke things".
- Self-rating: "Exceptional leader", "Outstanding advisor", "Top-quartile performer".
- Hedging that lowers the institutional register: "Helped to lead", "Was part of the team that", "Contributed to the success of".
- Outcome claims without scope: "Delivered significant value" without naming the value, the scope, or the client tier.
- Aggressive metrics framing: "Smashed quota by 130%" — translate to "Outperformed target by 30 per cent against agreed plan" in this register.

---

## Preferred forms

- **Institutional role language.** "Lead advisor", "Principal counsel", "Engagement partner", "Steering committee chair", "Co-head of practice".
- **Scope anchoring with named magnitudes.** "Combined balance sheets €40bn+", "Forty-person practice", "Six jurisdictions", "Three regulatory authorities".
- **Substantive verbs.** Led, Chaired, Co-led, Founded, Established, Coordinated, Served as, Represented, Advised on, Sustained.
- **Formal scope phrasing.** "Direct responsibility for", "Principal relationship for", "Primary advisor to", "Engagement lead on".
- **Period-appropriate scope.** Cite scope at the period when it was held, not aggregated lifetime numbers.

## JD signals that suggest this style

Recommend `prestige-structured` when the JD shows any of:

- Senior partner, managing director, head of, chair of, group director role titles.
- Firms with formal partner / non-partner career tracks (top-tier consulting, finance, law).
- Board advisory, executive search, senior advisory roles.
- JDs with reference to specific named clients, transaction sizes, or regulatory bodies.
- Roles where institutional scope (AUM, balance sheet, headcount, jurisdictions) is the primary differentiator.

Do **not** recommend `prestige-structured` when:

- The candidate is below senior level (use `achievement-driven` at senior, `measured-professional` below).
- Technical individual-contributor roles — even at senior IC level the register reads as overdressed (use `precision-formal` or `achievement-driven`).
- Startup or growth-stage companies (use `achievement-driven`).
- Academic or research roles (use `research-formal`).

## Compression behaviour

This style compresses badly. Institutional weight is in the substance and the length; trimming bullets to 12 words produces a register mismatch.

When over budget:

1. Drop the lowest-priority bullet entirely; do not shorten kept bullets below 17 words.
2. Profile compresses sentence 3, then trims sentence 2 of secondary scope markers.
3. `core_competencies` reduces from 7 rows to 5 before changing format.
4. `selected_outcomes` reduces from 6 to 4 bullets before any single bullet shortens.

Compression priorities to preserve: scope indicators, institutional vocabulary, P&L or AUM metrics, jurisdictional markers, formal role titles.

## Tone-chip compatibility

Style defaults to `["institutional", "polished", "scope-heavy"]`. Additional compatible chips: `formal`, `scope-anchored`, `scope-defined`, `concrete`.

Conflicts: never accept `restrained`, `brief`, `conversational`, `speculative`, `narrative` (the style is structured, not narrative).
