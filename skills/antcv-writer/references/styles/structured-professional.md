# Style: Structured Professional

**Primary constraint.** Process-led framing. Name the method and the scope, then the result.

**Row in style-matrix.md.** Density `medium`. Words per bullet 12 – 18. Profile 260 – 340 chars. ATS-Modern native-safe.

---

## What this style sounds like

Disciplined. Method-named. Every bullet says what was done in terms of the process the candidate ran, the framework applied, or the methodology used — then the outcome. The voice signals that the candidate can run a structured process, not just produce a result. Frameworks are named explicitly: ASPICE, ISO 9001, Six Sigma, ITIL, PRINCE2, Scaled Agile, COBIT, FMEA.

This style fits operations, quality, compliance, regulatory affairs, change governance, programme management, internal audit, and any role where the hiring decision turns on whether the candidate can run a disciplined process. It does not fit roles where impact framing is what gets read first (use `achievement-driven`) or roles where narrative is the register (use `context-rich`).

The audience is a reader who values method over magnitude. A bullet that names ASPICE specifically outperforms a bullet about "improving quality processes" — for this audience, the named framework is the signal.

## Section-by-section

### `profile`

Two to three sentences. 260 – 340 characters. Lead with role and the methodological surface the candidate operates on.

- Sentence 1: role and the methodological framework or process domain (8 – 12 words).
- Sentence 2: how the candidate operates the process — what kinds of organisations, what kinds of audits or governance bodies.
- Sentence 3 (optional): current focus or framework specialisation.

**Good:**

> Change governance specialist with twelve years applying ASPICE and ISO 26262 across automotive supplier programmes. Runs structured change boards, requirements traceability frameworks, and pre-board screening workflows for multi-vendor customer integration. Currently coordinating Automotive SPICE assessments for next-generation perception programmes.

**Avoid:** opening with outcome framing ("Drove 40% efficiency gain"). Process descriptions without named frameworks ("ran quality programmes").

### `core_competencies`

Two columns, `table-grid` format. 5 – 7 rows. Focus area named as a methodology or process domain; expertise names the specific framework, scope, and outcome where natural.

**Good:**

| Focus area | Strategic expertise |
|---|---|
| ASPICE governance | Pre-board screening framework adopted across three customer programmes |
| Functional safety | ISO 26262 assessor; led two re-certifications |
| Change governance | Multi-vendor change boards, structured CCR workflow |
| Requirements traceability | Hardware-software interface specifications across optical and ECU teams |
| Quality systems | Six Sigma Black Belt; FMEA and DFMEA review cycles |

**Avoid:** generic focus areas without named methodology ("Project management"). Bullets that describe outcomes without naming the process.

### `selected_outcomes`

3 – 5 bullets. Each title 4 – 7 words (named method + outcome); body 12 – 18 words (method, scope, result).

**Good:**

- **ASPICE pre-board screening adopted by three programmes.** Designed the framework, piloted on one customer programme, then extended to two downstream programmes after measurable cycle-time improvement.
- **Two ISO 26262 re-certifications with zero major findings.** Coordinated the preparation across optical, electrical, and software teams; closed all open findings before formal assessment.
- **Customer change request workflow standardised across two tier-1s.** Documented the workflow in alignment with ASPICE and the tier-1's internal governance; adoption signed off by both customer change boards.

**Avoid:** outcome-only titles ("Cut review cycles 40%" — that title belongs to `achievement-driven`). Bullets that name the result without naming the method that produced it.

### `experience`

3 bullets per role. Each 12 – 18 words. Past tense for past, present for current. Bullet 1 names the role and methodological scope; bullet 2 names a specific action anchored in a framework; bullet 3 names the outcome.

**Good (Change Control Lead, Innoviz, 2020 – 2025):**

- Led ASPICE-aligned change governance across three automotive tier-1 customer programmes, owning the customer change request workflow end-to-end.
- Designed and rolled out pre-board screening framework for change requests, adopted by two downstream programmes after initial pilot.
- Supported two ISO 26262 re-certifications with no major findings; reduced average review cycle time by 40 per cent.

**Avoid:** "Was responsible for change management" — name the framework. Outcome bullets without the methodology that produced them.

### `tools_methods`

This section is particularly important in `structured-professional`. Group by methodological domain rather than tool-type.

**Good:**

- **Governance frameworks:** ASPICE, ISO 26262, ISO 9001, COBIT, ITIL v4
- **Quality methods:** Six Sigma Black Belt, FMEA, DFMEA, design reviews
- **Project methods:** Stage-gate, PRINCE2, structured Agile

### Other sections

Certifications: prominent in this style — a `credential-forward` adjacent placement. Education and publications follow `cv-skeleton.md` defaults.

## Banned forms specific to structured-professional

In addition to the global banned word/phrase lists, this style rejects:

- Hero-narrative framing: "Led the transformation", "Drove the change". Use the framework name instead.
- Outcome-only bullets without method: "Cut cycle time 40%" reads as outcome-only. Pair with the method that produced the result.
- Vague verbs: "Handled compliance", "Worked on quality". Name the specific framework and specific activity.
- Process claims without scope: "Implemented quality processes". Specify which process, across what scope, applied by which framework.
- Acronym soup without anchoring: "Applied ASPICE, ISO, COBIT across teams". Name the specific framework used for the specific work.

---

## Preferred forms

- **Framework-named bullets.** "ASPICE-aligned change governance" beats "structured change governance".
- **Scope-defined verbs.** "Coordinated across optical, electrical, and software teams" beats "Coordinated across teams".
- **Methodology specificity.** Six Sigma Black Belt, not Six Sigma. ASPICE Level 2 assessor, not ASPICE familiar. ISO 26262 ASIL-D scope, not functional safety.
- **Process-shape verbs.** Designed, rolled out, piloted, standardised, certified, audited, assessed, governed.
- **Quantified findings.** "Two re-certifications, zero major findings" — finding counts are the right metric for this style, not revenue or efficiency.

## JD signals that suggest this style

Recommend `structured-professional` when the JD shows any of:

- Named methodology requirements: "ASPICE Level 2 required", "ISO 13485 experience", "Six Sigma Black Belt preferred".
- Operations, quality, compliance, internal audit, regulatory affairs role titles.
- Change management or change governance roles, especially in regulated industries.
- Programme management roles where the framework matters as much as the outcome (defence, medical devices, automotive functional safety).
- JDs with a section listing required frameworks rather than required outcomes.

Do **not** recommend `structured-professional` when:

- The role is outcome-led commercial (use `achievement-driven`).
- The candidate's history lacks named methodologies — the style will read as performative.
- Academic or research roles (use `research-formal`).
- The JD is in narrative form without naming frameworks (use `measured-professional`).

## Compression behaviour

When over budget:

1. Bullet bodies compress before bullets drop. The method name and scope stay; surrounding context trims.
2. Profile compresses sentence 3 first; sentence 2 keeps its methodology naming.
3. `tools_methods` is sacrosanct — do not drop framework names to save space.
4. `experience` bullet 2 (the specific action) compresses before bullets 1 (scope) or 3 (outcome).

Compression priorities to preserve: methodology names, scope indicators, framework references, finding counts, certification levels.

---

## Tone-chip compatibility

Style defaults to `["disciplined", "method-led", "scope-defined"]`. Additional compatible chips: `precise`, `credentialed`, `named-methodology`, `technical`, `factual`.

Conflicts: never accept `narrative`, `why-led`, `outcome-led` (re-orders the bullet against the primary constraint), `speculative`, `conversational`.
